## Check color contrast before it ships

A color contrast checker compares a foreground color against a background color and reports the contrast ratio. Designers and developers use that ratio to decide whether body text, headings, buttons, icons, and form labels are readable enough for accessibility requirements.

This checker focuses on the common WCAG contrast thresholds. It calculates the ratio locally in your browser, marks each threshold as pass or fail, and gives a practical color suggestion when the pair needs more contrast.

## WCAG contrast thresholds

The common thresholds are:

- `4.5:1` for WCAG AA normal text.
- `7:1` for WCAG AAA normal text.
- `3:1` for WCAG AA large text.
- `4.5:1` for WCAG AAA large text.
- `3:1` for UI components and graphical objects.

Large text usually means at least 18pt regular text or 14pt bold text. UI component checks can apply to important boundaries, icons, focus indicators, and controls.

## Color contrast examples

- Blue interface text on a pale notebook-paper background.
- Red error text on a light red warning panel.
- Muted gray helper copy below a form input.
- White button text on a saturated brand color.
- A disabled-looking label that may be too faint to read.

## How to fix a failing color pair

When a pair fails, change one side of the pair until the ratio rises above the threshold you need. In practice, that usually means making the foreground darker on a light background, making the foreground lighter on a dark background, or choosing a less subtle gray for small text.

This tool suggests a nearby foreground color when possible. Treat the suggestion as a starting point and review the final color in the real interface where spacing, font weight, and surrounding colors can affect perception.

## What this checker does not prove

Passing color contrast is one important accessibility step, but it is not a full accessibility audit or legal compliance certificate. A page can pass contrast and still have issues with keyboard access, labels, focus order, alt text, motion, or screen reader structure.

## Privacy and trust

Color checks run locally in the browser. Raw foreground and background values are not collected in analytics events.
